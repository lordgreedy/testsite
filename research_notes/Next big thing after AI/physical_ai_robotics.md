# Physical AI: Humanoid & Industrial Robotics, Autonomous Vehicles/Robotaxis, Drones & Defense Autonomy (as of November 2026)

Research note compiled 2026-11-18. Method: web search across ~17 queries. Several primary sites (forbes.com, humanoidsdaily.com) were unreachable from this session's network, so some figures rest on search-result summaries of those pages, not full reads. Where sources conflict, both figures are given. Treat aggregator/blog figures as directional.

## 1. Humanoid robots: funding, unit shipments, prices, and 2030/2035 forecasts

### Takeaway
Humanoid shipments are real but still small: about 19,000–25,000 units worldwide in H1 2026 (roughly 3–4x year over year), and Chinese makers (AgiBot, Unitree, UBTech) ship about 90–95% of them. US leaders (Figure, Agility, Apptronik, 1X, Tesla) have far higher valuations but ship only hundreds of units, mostly into pilots or their own fleets. Bank forecasts were raised sharply in 2026 (Goldman now sees about 6.5M units and about $138B by 2035), but they remain forecasts.

- Morgan Stanley: humanoid market about $5T by 2050 (incl. supply chain/services) and >1B humanoids in use by 2050. Secondary sources cite about 13M units in service by 2035 — [Morgan Stanley](https://www.morganstanley.com/insights/articles/humanoid-robot-market-5-trillion-by-2050); [Humanoid.guide](https://humanoid.guide/morgan-stanley-about-humanoid-robots/)

### Inferences
- Goldman's 2026 estimate (75k) implies about 3x H1 2026 run-rate, which is plausible if H2 is seasonally stronger. Real 2026 volume is likely about 50k–75k units, still about 1/10 of annual industrial-robot installations.
- The "deployment" gap is sharp. China leads in units (often cheaper research/education/entertainment robots such as the Unitree G1). US firms lead in valuation and in factory pilots, but few of their robots do productive commercial work at scale. Many Chinese units go to research, education, demos and government-linked buyers rather than productive labor. The Humanoid Analytics piece's title ("Where are they working?") points to this.
- Valuation to shipments: Figure (~$39B) and Tesla's narrative are priced on 2030s outcomes, not current revenue.

### Gaps
- I could not verify Unitree R1 pricing (widely reported as about $5,900 at its mid-2025 launch) or the G1's (~$16k) list price from a fetched source in this session. Systematic humanoid price-decline data (e.g., BOM cost curves) was also not found.
- No audited shipment numbers for Figure, Agility, Apptronik or 1X. Tesla has disclosed no Optimus unit counts.
- Full IFR humanoid/service-robot statistics were not retrieved.

## 2. Industrial robot installations (IFR World Robotics 2025 and 2026)

### Takeaway
Industrial robotics is the mature, already-scaled part of physical AI: about 603k installs in 2025 (+11%) and about 5M in operational stock. China installs 59% of the world total and increasingly uses domestic suppliers.

### Cited Findings
- WR 2025 (2024 data): 542,076 global installations. China installed 295,000 (54% of global, a record, +7%). Chinese suppliers' share of China's domestic installations rose from 30% (2020) to 57% (2024) — [IFR WR 2025 press release](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/global-robot-demand-in-factories-doubles-over-10-years); [IFR China release](https://ifr.org/downloads/press_docs/2025-09-25-IFR_press_release_China_in_English.pdf)
- WR 2026 (released Sep 24 2026, 2025 data): installations >600,000 (+11%; one guide cites 603,307). Operational stock about 5M (+9%). China installed 354,200 (59%), with stock about 2M — [IFR "Five Million Robots"](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/five-million-robots-now-operate-in-factories-globally); [Inside Deep Tech](https://www.insidedeeptech.com/ifr-world-robotics-2026-603k-installs-five-million-stock-full-guide/)
- US 2025: 38,400 installs (+12%), passing Japan as #2 market. South Korea 30,000 (-1%). India about 10,500 — [IFR US press release, Sep 24 2026](https://ifr.org/downloads/press_docs/EN-2026-SEP-24-IFR_Press_Release_WR-USA.pdf)
- IFR forecast: 655,000 installs in 2026 and 806,000 in 2029 — [Design Engineering](https://www.design-engineering.com/ifr-releases-world-robotics-2026-report-1004049481/)
- China's NBS data reportedly confirms China makes and installs about 59% of world industrial robots — [TechTimes, Oct 26 2026](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/329482/20261026/china-now-makes-installs-59-world-industrial-robots-nbs-data-confirms.htm)

### Inferences
- Industrial robot growth (about 5–11%/yr) is steady, not explosive. The "wave" thesis rests on newer categories (humanoids, AVs, drones) layered on top of this base. China's 59% share of installs plus its about 90%+ share of humanoid units suggests China leads physical-AI manufacturing scale.

### Gaps
- Service/professional robot data from WR 2026 was not retrieved.

## 3. Robotaxis: Waymo, Tesla, Baidu Apollo Go, Zoox, Pony.ai, WeRide

Robotaxis have crossed into real commercial scale. Waymo does about 500k+ paid rides/week across about 10+ US cities (about 2x YoY), Apollo Go has >20M cumulative rides, and Pony and WeRide each run about 2,000 vehicles. Tesla is expanding quickly in city count but from a tiny unsupervised base. Waymo's stated 1M/week by end-2026 target looks at risk: it has published no new weekly figure since spring.

## 4. Drones and defense autonomy

### Takeaway
Defense autonomy is where physical AI is scaling fastest in both capital and units. Ukraine produces millions of cheap FPV drones per year. The Pentagon's FY2026 request created the first dedicated autonomy line ($13.4B). Private valuations re-rated sharply in 2026: Anduril at $61B (talks at about $100B), Helsing at $18B, Shield AI at $12.7B.

### Cited Findings
- Anduril: $5B Series H at $61B valuation (May 2026, led by Thrive and a16z), about 2x its $30.5B a year earlier. - Defense-tech valuations (Anduril $61B or more) now rival or exceed the leading humanoid startups. Capital is flowing to physical AI with a government buyer.

## 5. Bottlenecks and skeptical views

### Takeaway
The main constraints are dexterous manipulation and the lack of touch/robot-interaction data, actuator/hand hardware, unit economics, and the gap between demos and productive work. Skeptics (notably Rodney Brooks) argue humanoid dexterity will lag for a decade-plus. Tesla's repeated Optimus slippage illustrates the hype gap.

### Cited Findings
- Rodney Brooks (Sept 2025 essay and Fortune coverage): humanoids will struggle with dexterity because there is no large corpus of touch data comparable to text or vision. He doubts that learning from human video will solve it — [Fortune, Sep 29 2025](https://fortune.com/2025/09/29/rodney-brooks-mit-roboticist-irobot-roomba-optimus-tesla-figure-optimus-robots); [TechCrunch, Oct 10 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/10/the-world-is-just-not-quite-ready-for-humanoids-yet/)
- Brooks's Jan 1 2026 scorecard: deployable dexterity will remain "pathetic" vs. human hands beyond 2036. He expects successful robots in about 15 years to look non-humanoid (wheels, multiple arms) — [rodneybrooks.com blog](https://rodneybrooks.com/blog/)
- Optimus timeline: announced 2021, with introduction once promised for 2023. In mid-2026 it was still not doing productive labor publicly, and many demos were teleoperated (per secondary reviews). Tesla's 10-Q still describes it as pre-scale — [nasrtech review](https://www.nasrtech.dev/blog/humanoid-robots-2026-hype-vs-reality/); [Tesla 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001318605/000162828026049270/tsla-20260630.htm)
- Safety and readiness: TechCrunch (Oct 2025) reported industry experts saying humanoid safety standards and deployment readiness are not there yet — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/10/the-world-is-just-not-quite-ready-for-humanoids-yet/)
- Robotaxi skepticism: Tesla's unsupervised fleet stayed at about 20 vehicles into June 2026 despite repeated scale claims — [Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/06/03/tesla-robotaxi-expands-entire-austin-metro-only-20-vehicles/). Waymo's economics remain loss-making (Other Bets -$1.8B in Q2 2026) — [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/10/11/alphabet-is-the-robotaxi-stock-to-buy-as-waymo-chases-1-million-rides-a-week/)

### Inferences
- The evidence supports a split verdict. Physical AI is a real, scaling wave in robotaxis (Waymo, Apollo Go), industrial robots (steady), and military drones (explosive). Humanoids are still at the pilot/demo stage outside China's lower-end units. Bank forecasts (Goldman 6.5M units by 2035) assume dexterity and data problems are solved, which skeptics dispute.
- China leads on volume (industrial installs, humanoid units, supply chain). The US leads on frontier AV deployment (Waymo) and defense-tech capital.

### Gaps
- I found no quantitative 2026 data on actuator/harmonic-reducer supply constraints, battery runtime limits (typically about 2–4 hrs for humanoids, unverified here), or rare-earth magnet exposure.
- No 2026 update on humanoid safety standards (e.g., ISO 25785-1 progress) or NHTSA AV framework changes was retrieved.
- Robot foundation-model data efforts (NVIDIA GR00T/Cosmos, Physical Intelligence π0, Google Gemini Robotics, teleoperation data farms) and their funding were not covered in this session's searches.
