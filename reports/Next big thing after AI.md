# AI's next boom is made of atoms

The data do not show an AI boom that is ending. They show AI moving out of software and into the physical world, and the next big things are mostly the pieces AI needs or makes possible there. As of 18 November 2026, AI *spending and revenue* are still speeding up. The four largest hyperscalers guide to about **$720–740B of 2026 capex**, roughly double 2025, and Nvidia's data-center revenue grew **110% year over year**. What has cooled is *markets and sentiment*: the Nasdaq is in correction, Nvidia's stock fell about 24% in two weeks, Oracle's credit is under strain, AI's share of venture dollars has fallen from about 80% to 64% since Q1, and Gartner puts generative AI in its "trough of disillusionment." So the premise "the AI boom is slowing" is half right. It describes a valuation and financing correction, not a fall in demand. Ranked by capital, policy support and commercial reality, the strongest candidates for the next wave are **(1) energy and grid infrastructure**, **(2) defense technology and autonomy**, **(3) the already-deployed parts of physical AI** (robotaxis, industrial robots and drones, with humanoids much later), **(4) space**, **(5) biotech, led by GLP-1 drugs that are already a mass market**, and **(6) quantum**, where the near-term business is post-quantum cryptography migration rather than quantum computers themselves. Historical cycles suggest that a correction moves value from the people building infrastructure to the people *deploying* it. The best preparation is therefore to pair AI fluency with a physical-world domain such as power, defense, robotics, health or security, rather than to bet on a single "post-AI" theme.

> **How to read the figures:** The underlying research could not reach several primary hosts (SEC, IEA, Crunchbase, PitchBook PDFs), so many numbers come from secondary summaries of primary reports. Single-source or unverified figures are marked **[unverified]**, and some trackers measure different things. Nothing here is personalized financial advice.

## AI spending keeps accelerating while AI stock prices correct

**Spending is not slowing.** After the Q3 reports, Amazon expects about **$220B** of 2026 cash capex and says it will "rise meaningfully" in 2027 ([DataStudios](https://www.datastudios.org/post/amazon-q3-2026-results-aws-39-percent-growth-46-billion-220-billion-capex)). Alphabet raised its 2026 range to **$195–205B** and expects capex to "increase significantly" in 2027 ([NextMSC](https://www.nextmsc.com/industry-deep-dive/hyperscaler-capex-comparison-2026)). Meta narrowed its range to $135–145B ([AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/amazon-microsoft-alphabet-meta-plan-725b-ai-capex-in-2026)). Together the four guide to about $720–740B, up from about $376–410B in 2025 and about $186B in 2024 ([Yield Theory tracker](https://www.yieldtheory.app/research/hyperscaler-ai-capex-tracker-2026); [Platformonomics](https://platformonomics.com/2025/10/follow-the-capex-q3-2025-scoreboard/)). The one soft spot is Microsoft. Its calendar-2026 figure was reportedly trimmed from about $190B to about $170B, and its quarterly capex missed consensus because of **delayed third-party GPU deliveries**, a supply problem rather than a demand one ([DataStudios](https://www.datastudios.org/post/microsoft-q1-fy27-results-azure-48-percent-33-2-billion-capex-45-2-billion); [Value Add VC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/big-tech-ai-capex-in-2025-microsoft-google-meta-amazon-and-the-spending-race)).

**Revenue is growing fast too.** Nvidia reported Q3 FY27 revenue of **$115.8B (+103%)** on 17 November and guided Q4 to $130B ([NVIDIA via GlobeNewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/11/17/3405200/0/en/nvidia-announces-financial-results-for-third-quarter-fiscal-2027.html)). Azure grew 48%. AWS grew 39%, its sixth straight quarter of acceleration, and has a $578B backlog ([DataStudios](https://www.datastudios.org/post/amazon-q3-2026-results-aws-39-percent-growth-46-billion-220-billion-capex)). Google Cloud's backlog stands at $560.8B per Alphabet's 10-Q ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001652044/000165204426000084/goog-20260930.htm)). Anthropic's annualized revenue reached $65B in July 2026, about 7x year over year ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/17/anthropic-says-annualized-revenue-climbed-to-65-billion-in-july.html)). OpenAI is reportedly at about $50B ([Value Add VC](https://valueaddvc.com/blog/openai-revenue-2026-20b-arr-4b-month-path-to-profitability)). These run-rates use different definitions, and higher figures for Anthropic (above $80B) are not reconciled with its reported quarterly revenue.

**Prices and financing are where it is cracking.** The Nasdaq closed more than 10% below its 6 October high in November ([Reuters via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/p-500-nasdaq-futures-slide-104351968.html)). Nvidia fell **23.8%** between 29 October and 13 November ([AIStockWire](https://aistockwire.com/blog/ai-stock-selloff-timeline-nvidia-nvda-huang-midterms-november-2026)) and barely moved after beating estimates. Reports that Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang resigned around 12 November **are unverified and conflict with other coverage**. Reuters-level reporting mentions only "uncertainty about future leadership," and this report does not treat the resignation as fact. The financing picture has changed in kind. Capex now exceeds operating cash flow at Meta (Q3 free cash flow about −$2.1B) and at Amazon (trailing free cash flow −$42.4B) ([Winzheng](https://www.winzheng.com/en/article/meta-q3-2026-capex-exceeds-operating-cash-flow); [Amazon Q3 recap](https://samberd.substack.com/p/amazon-q3-2026-a-better-business)). Hyperscaler investment-grade bond issuance reached about **$229B year to date versus $108B in all of 2025** ([Luminix](https://www.useluminix.com/reports/industry-analysis/when-will-the-ai-bubble-burst/source/2)). Oracle's 5-year CDS is about 295 bps, against 87 bps a year earlier, and it is on negative watch at BBB- ([BigGo Finance](https://finance.biggo.com/news/a99b8126-c0f9-4175-9b5d-c5d27112f018)). The BIS and IMF have both flagged circular AI financing, in which Nvidia funds labs that buy Nvidia chips, as a systemic risk ([Wikipedia: AI bubble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_bubble)).

**Enterprise payoff remains the weakest link.** McKinsey's 2026 survey finds AI scaling rising (44%, from 38%), but the share reporting any EBIT impact is flat at **37%**, and "high performers" are flat at about 6% ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai)). Census data put US business AI use at roughly 18–24% in 2026, about 37% at firms with 250 or more employees ([USA Times](https://usatimes.com/census-btos-ai-adoption-rate-firm-size-sector-2026/)). The often-quoted "95% of pilots fail" figure is 2025 data from a methodologically criticized MIT study ([RCR Wireless](https://www.rcrwireless.com/20250825/ai/7-takeaways-gen-ai-divide-mit)). Power is the other limit. An estimated 30–50% of large data centers due in 2026 face delays ([Latitude Media](https://www.latitudemedia.com/news/up-to-half-of-the-worlds-data-centers-may-be-delayed-this-year/)), and grid interconnection waits run 4–7 years in key hubs ([Data Center Knowledge](https://datacenterknowledge.com/energy-power-supply/ai-s-expansion-hits-the-limits-of-local-power)).

| Premise test: "the AI boom is slowing" | Verdict | Key datum |
|---|---|---|
| Infrastructure spending | **Does not hold**: accelerating | 2026 capex about $730B, about 2x 2025; 2027 guided higher |
| AI vendor and cloud revenue | **Does not hold**: accelerating | Nvidia DC +110%; AWS +39%; about $1.8T in cloud backlogs |
| Equity valuations | **Holds** | Nasdaq correction; Nvidia about −24% in two weeks |
| Credit and financing | **Holds**: stress building | Debt issuance about 2x; Oracle CDS about 295 bps; negative FCF |
| Venture share | **Partly holds** (timing-driven) | AI share 80% → 64% (Q1 → Q3), but up 14 pts on Q3 2025 |
| Enterprise ROI | **Holds**: stalled, not falling | EBIT impact flat at 37% (McKinsey) |
| Physical deployment | **Constrained by supply, not demand** | 30–50% of 2026 data centers delayed by power |

The fair summary is that **money and expectations have peaked before the infrastructure has.** In Yardeni's phrase, AI equities are "losing air very rapidly rather than bursting" ([USA Benefits Now](https://usabenefitsnow.com/is-the-ai-bubble-bursting/)). One caution on the venture data: much of the drop in AI's share came simply because OpenAI and Anthropic did not raise money in Q3 ([TechJack Solutions](https://techjacksolutions.com/ai-brief/ai-startup-funding-102b-q3-2026-crunchbase/)). Anthropic's IPO, set to debut on Nasdaq on 19 November at up to a $1.405T valuation per its price range, shifts AI fundraising from venture to public markets ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/11/13/anthropic-set-its-ipo-price-range-at-70-to-78-valuing-it-at-up-to-1405-trillion/)). Final pricing was not confirmed at the time of writing.

## Six candidates ranked: energy and defense lead, quantum and fusion trail

Every serious forecaster now lists AI's physical substrate next to AI itself. Gartner's 2026 strategic trends include physical AI ([Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/articles/top-technology-trends-2026)). MIT Technology Review's 2026 breakthroughs include hyperscale data centers, compact nuclear reactors, sodium-ion batteries, private space stations and humanoid robots ([MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/01/12/1130697/10-breakthrough-technologies-2026/)). McKinsey's frontier list puts robotics, energy, bioengineering and space beside agentic AI ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/tech-and-ai/our-insights/the-top-trends-in-tech-2025)). Capital is following. The non-AI 36% of venture money is going mainly to defense, space, nuclear and quantum.

The ranking below weighs four things: **capital momentum** (venture, IPO and strategic money), **policy demand** (budgets and mandates), **commercial reality today** (revenue, units shipped, megawatts) and **how independent the theme is from the AI cycle**. Confidence refers to the claim that each area becomes a major growth wave within the stated timeline, not to any investment outcome.

| Rank | Candidate | Strongest evidence | Main risk | Confidence | Realistic timeline |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Energy and grid** (storage, gas, nuclear restarts, geothermal, then SMRs) | Data centers are about half of US demand growth; record storage builds; uranium contract price at a record | Interconnection, transformers, permitting; demand depends on AI capex | **High** | Now to 2030 for grid, storage and restarts; SMRs from about 2030; fusion 2035+ |
| 2 | **Defense tech and autonomy** | $35.6–40.8B of VC in H1 2026; FY27 request includes about $54B for drones and autonomy; EU €150B SAFE | Budgets depend on reconciliation and politics; concentrated in a few firms | **High** | Now to 2030, already scaling |
| 3 | **Physical AI**, split into deployed vs. speculative | Robotaxis about 500k paid rides/week (Waymo); 603k industrial robot installs; millions of drones | Humanoid dexterity and economics; robotaxis still lose money | **High** for robotaxis, drones and industrial; **Low–medium** for humanoids | Robotaxis scale 2026–2030; general-purpose humanoids 2030s |
| 4 | **Space** | $686B space economy (+12%); Starlink revenue +83%; Starship reached orbit; Space Force budget surging | Concentrated in SpaceX; government-dependent | **Medium–high** | Starlink/D2C now; Starship economics 2027–2030 |
| 5 | **Biotech** (GLP-1 now; gene editing and AI drug discovery later) | Lilly guiding to $91–92B revenue; 11% of US adults on GLP-1s | Price erosion; Phase III risk for AI-discovered drugs | **High** for GLP-1; **Medium** for gene editing and AI-bio; **Low** for longevity | GLP-1 now; key AI-drug and editing readouts 2028+ |
| 6 | **Quantum** | PQC deadlines (2030/2035); McKinsey says QC revenue above $1B in 2025 | P/S multiples of 69–532x; fault tolerance unproven | **High** for PQC migration; **Low–medium** for quantum compute | PQC 2026–2035; fault-tolerant QC 2029–2033+ |

### Energy is the bottleneck AI cannot route around

Energy ranks first because the buyer and the demand already exist. The IEA's mid-2026 update expects global electricity demand to grow **3.6% in 2026 and 3.8% in 2027**, up from 3% in 2025 ([IEA](https://www.iea.org/reports/electricity-mid-year-update-2026/executive-summary)). It projects US demand to grow nearly 2% a year through 2030, with about half of the increase coming from data centers ([IEA](https://www.iea.org/reports/electricity-2026/demand)). Lawrence Berkeley National Lab projects US data centers will reach **11.8% of US electricity by 2030** (range 9.5–15.3%), against 4.4% in 2023 ([LBNL 2025 update](https://www.rtoinsider.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/data-center-energy-usage-2025-update.pdf)). The binding constraint is getting connected: about **2,060 GW** of generation and storage sat in US interconnection queues at end-2025 ([Interconnection queue tracker](https://ahasignals.com/interconnection-queue-tracker/)).

Deployment is real and dated. US batteries installed a record 18.9–20.2 GWh in Q2 2026, with the range reflecting differing methodologies ([Wood Mackenzie](https://www.woodmac.com/press-releases/u.s.-energy-storage-market-posts-record-18.9-gwh-in-q2-as-longer-duration-systems-drive-growth); [Renewables Now](https://renewablesnow.com/news/us-battery-storage-installations-hit-record-20-2-gwh-in-q2-1300511/)). Fervo's Cape Station produced the world's **first utility-scale enhanced-geothermal power on 24 September 2026** ([Fervo](https://fervoenergy.com/fervo-energy-achieves-first-power-at-cape-station-a-landmark-moment-for-the-future-of-enhanced-geothermal-systems/)), though so far only one 33 MW block. TerraPower's Natrium received an NRC construction permit in March 2026 ([SMR Intel](https://smrintel.com/smr-nrc-approval-tracker/)), and Kairos's Hermes 2 reactor for Google broke ground in April, targeting **2030** operation ([Kairos Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kairos_Power)). Uranium term prices hit a record **$96/lb** in August ([Streetwise Reports](https://www.streetwisereports.com/article/2026/11/09/uranium-contract-prices-hit-a-record-as-two-explorers-ramp-up-drilling.html)). Nuclear startups took 4 of the 9 climate-tech rounds of $500M or more in Q3 ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/reports/q3-2026-climate-tech-report-abundant-megadeals-for-datacenter-adjacent-climate-technologies)).

Be realistic about timing. The first new nuclear megawatts for tech come from **restarts** such as Crane/TMI-1, targeted for 2027 ([PA Capital-Star](https://penncapital-star.com/energy-environment/concern-persists-as-crane-nuclear-plant-progresses-toward-2027-restart/)). First SMR power arrives around 2030. Multi-GW deals such as Meta's 6.6 GW are 2035 roadmaps ([Introl](https://introl.com/blog/meta-nuclear-power-deal-oklo-terrapower-vistra-6gw-2026)). Fusion raised a record **$4.48B** in the year to July 2026 ([FIA](https://www.fusionindustryassociation.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/FIA-Industry-report-2026_d9.pdf)), but no device has produced net electricity. CFS's SPARC first plasma has slipped to 2027, and Helion's 2028 Microsoft delivery is called "extremely aggressive" ([Discovery Alert](https://discoveryalert.com/analysis/nuclear-fusion-commercialisation-unproven-october-2026/)). The key caveat is that energy is the *least independent* of the candidates. Much of its demand case is AI capex, so a sharp capex cut would hit it. Even so, the IEA's demand growth also reflects EVs, cooling and electrification, which gives energy a floor that pure AI plays lack.

### Defense is the most AI-independent wave with a guaranteed buyer

Defense has the clearest combination of private capital, government demand and wartime proof of volume. PitchBook counts about **$35.6B** of defense and defense-adjacent venture funding in H1 2026 ([Tectonic Defense](https://www.tectonicdefense.com/defense-tech-funding-is-breaking-records-with-35-6b-invested-ytd/)). European defence tech raised **$7.4B in 2026 YTD, nearly 3x all of 2025** ([Tech.eu](https://tech.eu/2026/10/05/european-defencetech-hits-record-7-4b-as-investment-nearly-triples-in-2026/)). The US FY2027 request seeks about **$1.5T** in total, including about $53.6B for drones and autonomous systems and $17.5B for Golden Dome ([Defense News](https://www.defensenews.com/news/pentagon-congress/2026/04/21/pentagon-seeks-funds-for-golden-dome-drones-ai-in-largest-ever-budget-request/)). Final appropriations are not confirmed, and much of it depends on reconciliation. The EU has started disbursing its €150B SAFE defense loans ([European Commission](https://defence-industry-space.ec.europa.eu/commission-approves-first-wave-defence-funding-eight-member-states-under-safe-2026-01-15_en)). Strategic money confirms the trend: defense primes joined a record **$4.1B** of venture rounds in 2026 ([Oilprice](https://oilprice.com/Energy/Energy-General/Defense-Tech-Investment-Hits-Record-High-as-Arms-Giants-Ramp-Up-Startup-Funding.html)), and defense M&A deal count rose 56% in H1 ([JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/boots-off-the-ground-defense-tech-m-a-8359102/)). Valuations have re-rated. Anduril is at $61B ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/13/anduril-raises-5b-doubles-valuation-to-61b/)), Helsing at $18B ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/13/helsing-fund-raise-defense-18-billion.html)) and Shield AI at $12.7B. Ukraine shows the unit volumes: reportedly **6–7M small FPV drones in 2026** at about $300–400 each ([UNITED24](https://united24media.com/world/ukraine-builds-500000-strike-drones-a-month-more-than-the-us-plans-to-buy-in-a-year-21124)). That is orders of magnitude more units than all humanoids and robotaxis combined. The risks are concentration (Anduril's single round was about a third of Q2 defense funding) and political exposure. Democrats winning Congress on 3 November adds uncertainty to reconciliation-funded lines ([Reuters via Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/p-500-nasdaq-futures-slide-104351968.html)).

### Physical AI is two different stories: deployed now, and a decade out

Physical AI is where AI's next leg is most visible, but the category mixes mature and speculative markets. **Robotaxis are commercially real.** Waymo passed **500k paid rides a week** across 10 US cities in spring 2026, "doubling in less than a year" ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/27/waymo-skyrocketing-ridership-in-one-chart/); [Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/10/11/alphabet-is-the-robotaxi-stock-to-buy-as-waymo-chases-1-million-rides-a-week/)). Its end-2026 target of 1M a week looks at risk, since no new figure has been published since spring and a third-party model expects about 750k ([FutureSearch](https://futuresearch.ai/waymo-financial-forecast/)). Alphabet's Other Bets still lost $1.8B in Q2. Industrial robots are a steady base rather than a boom: about **603k installs in 2025 (+11%)**, 59% of them in China, and an IFR forecast of 655k in 2026 ([IFR](https://ifr.org/ifr-press-releases/news/five-million-robots-now-operate-in-factories-globally); [Design Engineering](https://www.design-engineering.com/ifr-releases-world-robotics-2026-report-1004049481/)).

**Humanoids are a forecast, not yet a market.** Estimated H1 2026 shipments of 19,100–25,000 units are growing about 3–4x, and about 90–95% come from Chinese makers, many going to research, education and demonstrations ([Humanoid Analytics](https://humanoidanalytics.com/2026/08/17/19100-humanoid-robots-shipped-in-six-months-where-are-they-working/)). Goldman raised its 2035 forecast to about **6.5M units and $138B** ([24/7 Wall St.](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/09/13/robots-everywhere-goldman-sachs-now-sees-6-5-million-humanoid-robots-by-2035/)). Against that, Tesla's own 10-Q still describes Optimus as in preparation for scale production ([Tesla 10-Q](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001318605/000162828026049270/tsla-20260630.htm)). Roboticist Rodney Brooks expects humanoid dexterity to remain "pathetic" beyond 2036, because no large touch-data corpus comparable to text exists ([rodneybrooks.com](https://rodneybrooks.com/blog/)). Figure's roughly $39B valuation against hundreds of delivered units prices in 2030s outcomes. Reports of an Agility SPAC listing, a Unitree STAR Market listing, Tesla's ramp to "several hundred" Optimus units a week, and a Dealroom robotics total of $55.8B are all **[unverified]**.

### Space has a cash engine; biotech already has its blockbuster

Space's case rests on one profitable flywheel plus defense demand. Starlink connectivity revenue rose from **$7.7B to $11.4B (+83%)** in 2025, and paid subscribers reached 10.3M at Q1 2026 per the S-1 ([Broadband Breakfast](https://broadbandbreakfast.com/spacex-ready-to-go-public-in-massive-ipo-reveals-that-starlink-is-paying-the-bills)). Starship V3 first reached orbit on 28 September 2026 and deployed 26 Starlink V3 satellites ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/28/spacex-prepares-to-send-starship-rocket-to-orbit-for-first-time.html)). The Space Foundation measured the 2025 space economy at **$685.6B (+12%)** ([Space Foundation](https://www.spacefoundation.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/SF-GSE-REPORT-2026July-FOR-RELEASE.pdf)), growing faster than the roughly 9% CAGR behind WEF/McKinsey's $1.8T-by-2035 forecast ([WEF](https://www.weforum.org/media/space-economy-set-to-triple-to-1-8-trillion-by-2035-new-research-reveals/)). The two use different scopes, so the comparison is directional only. The FY27 request roughly doubles the Space Force budget to about $71.2B, against a $185B Golden Dome program estimate ([Federal News Network](https://federalnewsnetwork.com/budget/2026/04/white-house-seeks-17-5-billion-for-golden-dome-but-most-funding-hinges-on-reconciliation)). Space venture and IPO totals of $36B in Q1 and $32B in Q2 ([Space Capital](https://www.spacecapital.com/reports/space-investment-quarterly-q2-2026)) are inflated by SpaceX's record June IPO. Its valuation is variously reported at $1.75T to about $2.3T **[conflicting]**. The sector is effectively a bet on one company plus government budgets.

Biotech's surprise is that its "next big thing" has already arrived. Lilly's Q3 2026 revenue was **$24.4B (+39%)**, and it raised FY2026 guidance to **$91–92B**, more than double its roughly $45B in 2024 ([Lilly 8-K](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0000059478/000005947826000104/q326lillysalesandearningsp.htm); [Zacks](https://www.zacks.com/stock/news/3018734/lly-stock-jumps-as-q3-earnings-beat-estimates-2026-sales-view-raised)). Gallup finds **11% of US adults** now take a GLP-1 for weight loss, up from 3% in 2024 ([Gallup](https://news.gallup.com/poll/712157/glp-usage-reaches-new-high.aspx)). Growth now comes from volume rather than price. Lilly's US volume rose 41% while realized price fell 7%, Medicare pays $245 a month, and TrumpRx cash prices start at $149 ([Kiplinger](https://www.kiplinger.com/retirement/medicare/medicare-to-cover-obesity-drugs-under-trump-deal); [KATV](https://katv.com/news/nation-world/trump-announce-deal-sell-weight-loss-drugs-149-trumprx-novo-nordisk-eli-lilly-wegovy-zepbound-ozempic-medicare-medicaid-report-pill-orforglipron-fda-rfk-jr-dr-oz)). Novo shows how fast leadership can flip: its US injectable Wegovy sales fell 13%, and its oral pill (about $810M in Q3) is holding up results ([TimesLive/Reuters](https://www.timeslive.co.za/news/world/2026-11-04-novo-nordisk-lifts-2026-outlook-as-wegovy-pill-surges/)). Lilly's oral Foundayo figure of about $184M is **[single-source]**. Further out, gene editing is real but small (Casgevy made $99M in Q3) ([CRISPR Medicine News](https://crisprmedicinenews.com/press-release-service/card/vertex-reports-third-quarter-2026-financial-results/)), with a potentially larger regulatory shift in the FDA's draft "plausible mechanism" pathway for bespoke editing therapies ([Clinical Trials Arena](https://www.clinicaltrialsarena.com/news/fda-unveils-new-pathway-to-usher-bespoke-therapies-to-market/)). AI drug discovery's real test is Insilico's rentosertib Phase III, a China-only trial that will not read out until about 2028 ([Clinical Research News](https://www.clinicalresearchnewsonline.com/news/2026/07/08/insilico-medicine-launches-phase-iii-trial-for-ai-developed-drug)). Biopharma venture funding is recovering rather than booming, at $23.9B year to date ([J.P. Morgan](https://www.jpmorgan.com/content/dam/jpmorgan/documents/cb/insights/outlook/jpm-biopharma-deck-q3-2026.pdf)). Longevity remains pre-clinical and funded by billionaires.

### Quantum's sure thing is a deadline, not a computer

Quantum ranks last as a growth wave but first for certainty on one item. NIST IR 8547, finalized on 27 October 2026, **deprecates RSA and ECC after 2030 and disallows them after 2035** ([CSA](https://labs.cloudsecurityalliance.org/research/csa-research-note-nist-ir8547-pqc-transition-20261030-csa-st/)). Every organization's cryptography migration is therefore mandatory, whether or not a useful quantum computer ever arrives. The compute side is real but early. McKinsey counts **$12.6B of 2025 start-up investment** and more than $1B of revenue, with up to $4.4B by 2028 ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/mckinsey-technology/our-insights/mckinsey-quantum-technology-monitor-2026-a-commercial-tipping-point)). IBM committed more than $10B and targets fault tolerance by 2029 ([IBM](https://newsroom.ibm.com/2026-06-02-ibm-commits-more-than-10-billion-to-quantum-computing,-funding-its-roadmap-from-todays-leading-systems-to-the-worlds-first-fault-tolerant-quantum-computers)). DARPA's benchmarking program is testing whether utility-scale quantum is possible at all by 2033 ([HPCwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/2026/10/07/atom-computing-diraq-ibm-and-ionq-advance-to-darpa-qbi-stage-c/)). Public markets have priced far ahead of this. As of late August, price-to-sales was about **69x for IonQ, 407x for Rigetti and 532x for D-Wave**, and IonQ fell about 39% over the year to late September ([Motley Fool](https://www.fool.com/investing/2026/10/20/ionq-rigetti-dwave-quantum-computing-shaking-wall-street-to-core-with-1-billion-warning/); [24/7 Wall St.](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/09/30/one-year-later-only-one-quantum-stock-call-paid-off-heres-what-changed/)). Quantinuum's reported 48 logical qubits is **[unverified]**.

The strongest signal across all six is where **policy from the US, EU and China overlaps**. China's 15th Five-Year Plan names quantum, embodied AI, fusion, biomanufacturing, BCI and 6G as "future industries" ([CGTN](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2026-03-05/China-to-foster-emerging-industries-and-future-technologies-1LgoSd9ePqE/p.html)). The US has taken about $27B of equity stakes in chip, rare-earth and quantum companies ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/07/26/the-u-s-government-invested-27-billion-in-corporate-stakes-good-luck-finding-them/)). Robotics, quantum, fusion and nuclear, and defense autonomy appear on every bloc's list.

## Busts move value from builders to deployers, and the lag runs to years

History argues against reading a market correction as the end of a technology. Carlota Perez's framework splits each technological revolution into a speculative **installation** period that ends in a financial bubble, a turning-point crisis, and a **deployment** "golden age" when the technology spreads through the economy ([IIPP/Medium](https://medium.com/iipp-blog/a-long-delayed-golden-age-or-why-has-the-ict-installation-period-lasted-so-long-b3471a0aaaeb)). Britain's 1840s Railway Mania was, in Andrew Odlyzko's words, "by many measures the greatest technology mania in history" and ended in one of the greatest crashes. Yet it left "a nationwide communication network of great utility," and it had prominent critics all along ([Odlyzko](https://www-users.cse.umn.edu/~odlyzko/doc/hallucinations.pdf)). The dot-com crash cut the Nasdaq from 5,048.62 to 1,139.90, a **78% fall**, and the index did not close above its 2000 peak until **April 2015, 15 years later** ([Phys.org/AP](https://phys.org/news/2015-04-years-nasdaq-recoups-losses-dot-com.pdf)). Amazon lost more than 90% peak to trough, then launched Prime, AWS and Kindle in 2005–2007 on top of the infrastructure the bubble had paid for ([Jon Erlichman](https://jonerlichman.substack.com/p/how-amazon-survived-the-dot-com-bust)).

Four lessons apply to 2026. **First, the people who finance the buildout at peak prices lose, and the people who use the leftover capacity win.** If AI capex overshoots, the result is cheap compute and power that later deployers will exploit, much as cheap fiber enabled cloud and mobile. **Second, the lag is long.** It took 5–7 years for the winners' breakout products and 15 years for the index. Skills in *applying* a technology pay off well before bets on its frenzy do. **Third, warning signs look like today's.** Debt replacing cash flow, vendor financing of customers, and new issues trading poorly are classic late-installation signals. Of 52 tracked 2026 US IPOs above $1B, **37 traded below their issue price** as of 16 November **[single-source; verify]** ([StockTitan](https://www.stocktitan.net/articles/biggest-ipos-2026-us-list)). **Fourth, timelines in every candidate area slip.** SPARC moved from 2025 to 2027, Optimus was first promised for 2023, Tesla's unsupervised robotaxi fleet stood at about 20 cars in June 2026 ([Electrek](https://electrek.co/2026/06/03/tesla-robotaxi-expands-entire-austin-metro-only-20-vehicles/)), and Isomorphic's first trials moved from end-2025 to end-2026. Plan on roadmap dates arriving two to three years late.

There is a real disagreement about where 2026 sits on this curve. One view: we are at the installation-phase frenzy now, the correction is the turning point, and deployment (agents, physical AI, AI in industry) is next. Gartner's placement of GenAI in the trough with a plateau within two years fits that reading ([FourWeekMBA](https://fourweekmba.com/ai-gartner-puts-genai-in-its-trough-with-3-5t-of-ai-exposure/)). The other view: capex is still doubling and guided higher for 2027, so the frenzy is not over, and a larger debt-financed peak may lie ahead. The data cannot settle which is right yet. The question to plan around is not "what replaces AI?" but "what does AI's deployment phase need, and who profits when the financing turns?"

## How individuals and teams should prepare

**Skills: combine AI fluency with a physical-world or regulated domain.** The best hard evidence on pay is PwC's 2026 barometer. Roles requiring AI skills carry an average **62% wage premium (up from 57%)**, and postings for them grew 69% versus 9% for the overall market ([PwC](https://www.pwc.com/gx/en/news-room/press-releases/2026/pwc-2026-ai-jobs-barometer.html)). PwC also finds human skills increasingly rewarded. LinkedIn's 2026 lists put AI literacy in the top five for both sales and marketing ([UCSD](https://career.rady.ucsd.edu/resources/linkedin-skills-on-the-rise-2026-sales/)). WEF's top three fastest-growing skills are AI and big data, networks and cybersecurity, and technological literacy ([WEF](https://www.weforum.org/publications/the-future-of-jobs-report-2025/digest/)). The ranking above points to specific combinations: AI plus **power systems and grid interconnection**; AI plus **robotics, controls and simulation**; AI plus **defense acquisition, export controls and security clearances**; AI plus **clinical and regulatory science**; and **applied cryptography and PQC migration**, which has a fixed 2030/2035 demand curve. The shortage in physical trades is real and measurable. Postings for electricians, welders and construction workers are up about 30% since 2022 ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/03/20/skilled-trade-demand-randstand-report-electricans-technicans-construction-workers-six-figure-salaries-data-center-boom/)), and Lightcast estimates a shortage of about 1.3M skilled-trades workers ([Staffing Industry Analysts](https://www.staffingindustry.com/news/global-daily-news/data-centers-retiring-workers-deepen-13-million-skilled-trades-shortage)). Wind turbine technicians are BLS's fastest-growing occupation (+49.9%), though from a base of only about 14,000 jobs ([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/emp/tables/fastest-growing-occupations.htm)).

**Careers: look for a named buyer and existing revenue.** Defense autonomy (government budgets), grid and storage (utility and hyperscaler contracts), robotaxis (paid rides) and GLP-1 manufacturing and distribution (a roughly $92B obesity-drug market in 2026 per IQVIA ([IQVIA](https://www.iqvia.com/ro-ro/locations/emea/blogs/2026/04/the-outlook-for-obesity-from-2026-to-2030))) all have customers today. Humanoids, fusion, fault-tolerant quantum and longevity are mostly venture-funded ahead of revenue. Joining them can be worthwhile, but treat that as an option-like career bet with a 5–10+ year horizon. If you join a well-funded startup in a hot sector, ask how long its cash lasts at current spend. The dot-com survivors had cash discipline, and a funding freeze is exactly what a correction brings.

**Teams: act on the certainties first.** Three moves are justified now whatever happens to AI stocks. First, **start a cryptographic inventory and PQC migration plan**, because NIST's deprecation of RSA and ECC after 2030 is fixed. Second, **build energy and compute cost into plans**. Power constraints are delaying 30–50% of large data centers, and about 20% of McKinsey respondents already say token and operating costs limit their AI use ([McKinsey](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/quantumblack/our-insights/the-state-of-ai)), so do not assume compute keeps getting cheaper on schedule. Third, **move AI work from pilots to measured P&L outcomes**. With EBIT impact flat at 37%, a correction will reward teams that can show returns and cut those that cannot. Watch vendor concentration too. An estimated 40–60% of Oracle's $523B backlog is tied to OpenAI **[single-source estimate]** ([TradingKey](https://www.tradingkey.com/analysis/stocks/us-stocks/261552887-Petar-Petrov)), so check how dependent your critical AI suppliers are on a few frontier labs' fundraising.

**Investing caveats (general, not personalized advice).** The data counsel caution about chasing any "next big thing" theme. Valuations in the hottest areas are far ahead of revenue: quantum pure plays at hundreds of times sales, Figure at about $39B with hundreds of robots delivered, Anduril re-rated 2x in a year. Concentration is extreme. The top 10 quantum deals took about 60% of 2025 funding ([Global Venturing](https://globalventuring.com/corporate/investment/nvidia-backs-three-quantum-startups-amid-investment-boom/)), and SpaceX dominates space totals. Several "post-AI" themes, notably energy, nuclear and robotics, are in practice **leveraged bets on continued AI capex**, so they do not diversify away from AI risk. Thematic ETFs in defense, uranium and quantum outperformed in the 12 months to January 2026 (VettaFi index +24% versus +20% for the Nasdaq-100) ([ETF Trends](https://www.etftrends.com/?p=635580)), but past thematic returns say little about future ones. The dot-com record is a reminder that even the index took 15 years to recover. Anyone making allocation decisions should consider their own horizon and diversification and consult a licensed adviser.

## Conclusion

The "next big thing after AI" framing gets the structure wrong. The 2026 data show no successor technology pulling capital *away* from AI. Instead, AI's own needs and capabilities are pulling capital into atoms: electrons for data centers, autonomy for weapons, perception for vehicles, and launch capacity for satellite networks. The truly AI-independent waves, defense driven by geopolitics and GLP-1s driven by biology, are the most robust to an AI correction, and one of them is already a mass market, with Lilly's tirzepatide alone running at roughly $64B a year. That changes the preparation question from "which theme do I jump to?" to "which physical or regulated domain will I bring AI into?" That position pays off whether AI capex keeps rising or the financing breaks, because history says the deployment phase belongs to people who apply cheapened infrastructure, not to those who paid peak prices for it.

The quiet takeaway is the gap between deadlines and roadmaps. The surest dates in this report are policy deadlines rather than technology milestones: NIST's 2030/2035 cryptography cutoffs, the FY27 budget cycle, Medicare's GLP-1 pricing. Technology timelines such as fusion first plasma, humanoid dexterity, fault-tolerant qubits and robotaxi targets have slipped almost without exception. Teams that anchor plans to the fixed policy dates and treat technology roadmaps as options will be early where it counts and less exposed where the hype runs ahead.
